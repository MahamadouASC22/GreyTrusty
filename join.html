Why advisors join

The good parts of the job, without the rest

You're here to fix things and explain them well. Everything around that — finding clients, chasing quotes, invoicing, reminding people — is ours to carry.

You set the price

Your hourly rate is yours to name and change whenever you like. Most advisors here sit between $50 and $95. We never discount your work to win a booking.

Your own hours

Weekday days, evenings, weekends, virtual-only — entirely your call. Empty slots are never sold on your behalf, and blackout dates take one click.

Matched, never auctioned

Clients answer a questionnaire and see you because your strengths, personality, and availability fit theirs. No racing to underbid anyone.

Scheduling handled

Clients book real openings from your calendar. Confirmations, reminders, and reschedules go out automatically, so your phone stops being a booking system.

Paid weekly, no invoicing

Clients are charged through the platform after the session. Your share lands weekly with a plain statement of every visit — no chasing anyone for a cheque.

Reputation that travels

Reviews attach to your profile and your specialties, so being genuinely excellent at printers or networking is something clients can actually find you for.

What we look for

Skill matters. So does patience.

Requirements

  • Three or more years of hands-on IT or technology support work
  • Two professional references we can speak to
  • A background check — we run it and pay for it
  • Own transport if you offer in-person visits
  • Liability insurance, or willingness to arrange it
  • A device and connection good enough for screen-share work

What makes an application stand out

  • You can explain something technical without using a single acronym
  • You're comfortable telling a client they don't need to buy anything
  • You write things down for people, unprompted
  • You have a specialty you're genuinely known for
  • You want the same clients for years, not a stream of one-offs
  • You're patient with people who are embarrassed to ask
